# Data Acquisition

## MICA-PNI dataset
Images of the MICA-PNI dataset were acquired at the McConnell Brain Imaging Centre of the Montreal Neurological Institute on a 7 T Siemens Terra scanner with a 32-channel receive and 8-channel transmit head coil operated in parallel-transmission (pTx) mode. **Quantitative T1** (qT1) images were acquired with a three-dimensional magnetization-prepared two rapid gradient-echo (MP2RAGE) sequence (Marques et al., 2010) using universal pulses to improve B1+ homogeneity (Gras et al., 2017). Imaging parameters were: 0.5 mm isotropic resolution; matrix size, 320 × 488 × 520; repetition time (TR), 5,170 ms; echo time (TE), 2.81 ms; inversion times (TI1/TI2), 1,000/3,200 ms; flip angles (α1/α2), 4°/4°; in-plane GRAPPA acceleration factor, 3; partial Fourier factor, 0.75; field of view (FOV), 160 × 244 × 260 mm³. These images were used for cortical surface reconstruction, intracortical microstructural profiling, microstructural profile covariance, and geodesic distance. **Resting-state functional MRI** data were acquired with a multi-echo T2*-weighted gradient-echo echo-planar imaging (GRE-EPI) sequence (Center for Magnetic Resonance Research, University of Minnesota). Imaging parameters were: 1.9 mm isotropic resolution; matrix size, 118 × 118; 75 slices in an oblique axial orientation tilted relative to the AC–PC line; TR, 1,690–1,706 ms; TE1/TE2/TE3, 10.8/27.3/43.8 ms; flip angle, 67°; FOV, 224 × 224 mm²; partial Fourier factor, 0.75; multiband acceleration factor, 3; GRAPPA acceleration factor, 3. During the 7 min 45 s acquisition (275 volumes), participants were instructed to fixate on a gray cross and to rest. All MRI data were processed with micapipe v0.2.0 (Cruces et al., 2022).

**Participants.** Healthy controls of the MICA-PNI cohort were included if they had also been scanned with the MICA-MICs 3 T protocol described below and if all of their data were available. These comprised the qT1 intensity profiles (sampled on the fsLR-32k and fsLR-5k surfaces) and the microstructural profile covariance, geodesic-distance and resting-state functional connectivity matrices of the 7 T session (a1), and the structural connectome and streamline-length matrices of the 3 T session. Twenty-five participants met these criteria and formed the sample of all MRI analyses (Figs 1–3).

## MICA-MICs dataset
Whole-brain structural connectivity was estimated in the same participants from the openly available MICA-MICs dataset (Royer et al., 2022). Images were acquired at the McConnell Brain Imaging Centre of the Montreal Neurological Institute on a 3 T Siemens Magnetom Prisma-Fit scanner equipped with a 64-channel head-and-neck coil. **Diffusion-weighted images** were acquired with a multi-shell spin-echo echo-planar imaging (SE-EPI) protocol comprising three shells — b = 300 s/mm² (10 directions), b = 700 s/mm² (40 directions) and b = 2,000 s/mm² (90 directions) — each preceded by a b = 0 image (143 volumes in total), together with a reverse phase-encoded (posterior-to-anterior) b = 0 acquisition to correct susceptibility-induced distortions. Imaging parameters were: 1.6 mm isotropic resolution; matrix size, 140 × 140; TR, 3,500 ms; TE, 64.4 ms; flip angle, 90°; refocusing flip angle, 180°; FOV, 224 × 224 mm²; partial Fourier factor, 0.75; multiband acceleration factor, 3; in-plane GRAPPA acceleration factor, 2. These data were used to reconstruct whole-brain tractography and structural connectomes with micapipe v0.2.0.

## BigBrain dataset
BigBrain is an ultrahigh-resolution three-dimensional reconstruction of a sectioned, cell-body-stained postmortem human brain from a 65-year-old woman (Amunts et al., 2013), obtained from the open-access BigBrain repository (https://bigbrain.loris.ca/main.php). The paraffin-embedded brain was sectioned coronally into 7,404 sections of 20 μm, stained for cell bodies with the Merker method, and digitized. After manual inspection for artifacts (such as rips, tears, shears and stain crystallization), sections were repaired automatically by nonlinear alignment to a postmortem MRI of the same brain acquired before sectioning, followed by intensity normalization and block averaging. The three-dimensional reconstruction was implemented with a successive coarse-to-fine hierarchical procedure. The publicly released volume at 100 μm resolution was used. Intensities were inverted, so that higher values reflect greater cellular density and soma size.

## AHEAD dataset
The AHEAD dataset is a multimodal three-dimensional reconstruction of two postmortem human brains at 200 μm resolution, which integrates ultra-high-field MRI and light microscopy (Alkemade et al., 2022). Microscopy images were obtained from coronal sections at 200 μm intervals (approximately 800 sections per brain) stained for cell bodies (Nissl, thionin), nerve fibers (Bielschowsky silver impregnation), and parvalbumin, calretinin and calbindin (immunohistochemistry). Blockface and microscopy images were co-registered with the ANTs SyN algorithm to minimize nonlinear distortions and to align brain boundaries and vascular landmarks. Small intensity variations in the reconstruction were adjusted linearly, preserving the original dynamic range of each stain. One brain of the dataset, from a 59-year-old woman (https://doi.org/10.21942/uva.16844500.v1), was used, and only the Bielschowsky and parvalbumin stains were analyzed, which label nerve fibers and parvalbumin-expressing interneurons, respectively.

## MNI open iEEG atlas
The MNI Open iEEG Atlas (discovery cohort; Frauscher et al., 2018) is a multicenter, openly available collection of intracranial recordings acquired during different states of vigilance. It comprises recordings from 106 patients with drug-resistant epilepsy (48 women; mean ± standard deviation [SD] age, 33.4 ± 10.6 years; range, 13–62 years), totaling 1,772 channels in non-epileptic tissue mapped to a common stereotaxic space and distributed at a sampling rate of 200 Hz. The present study used the segments recorded during resting wakefulness with eyes closed. All data were collected during standard clinical protocols at the participating sites.

## MICA iEEG dataset
Intracranial EEG data were obtained at the MICA laboratory from 38 patients with drug-resistant epilepsy who underwent presurgical evaluation. Recordings were acquired during resting wakefulness with eyes closed and during N2 and N3 sleep from the same 5,065 bipolar channels, sampled at 2,000 Hz, and organized in BIDS format. Sensitivity maps of the electrode contacts were derived from subject-specific leadfield models computed with the electroMICA pipeline. These maps were available for 37 of the 38 patients; the channels of the remaining patient carried no surface sensitivity. The combination of contact sensitivities into bipolar-channel sensitivities on the fsLR-32k surface (32,492 vertices per hemisphere) is described in [Figure 2](figure_2.md#surface-mapping-of-the-mica-recordings-fig-2b).
